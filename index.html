        Takeaway

        What to notice

        • 1

          Choices multiply. Parts only add.

          Four modules with 4, 3, 2 and 4 options make 4 × 3 × 2 × 4 = 96 products from 4 + 3 + 2 + 4 = 13 kinds of part. That is how a modular maker offers variety without filling a warehouse.

        • 2

          Unit cost can go either way.

          In electronics, sealing parts in is the cheap end. In furniture and cabinets, modular is the cheap end, because standard flat parts are cheap to make, ship and store.

        • 3

          Each side wins something.

          Modular wins on choice, repair and stock. Sealed wins on looks, fit and keeping out water and dust, and brand-only parts can keep repair sales with the maker. Even where modular comes out ahead overall, the trade-off is real.

        • 4

          Waiting is the other way to avoid stock.

          Made-to-order furniture and custom cabinets carry little finished stock too, but the customer waits. Shared parts give low stock risk and a short wait together.

        About the scores. Every score on this page (flexibility out of 10, costs and benefits out of 5) is an illustrative judgment based on the reason printed beside it. They are for comparing positions, not measurements. The option counts (4, 3, 2 and 4) and the 3D models are teaching examples, not catalogue data. Facts about real companies carry a citation. Between two anchors, scores are blended in a straight line from the nearest two.
